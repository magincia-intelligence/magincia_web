## Top of the brief
- **English Australia CEO Aird at The PIE Live Asia Pacific Day 2: ELICOS sector is now 20% smaller than it was 20 years ago, with a 60% visa grant rate against 96% for Chinese degree students and a $2,050 fee equal to 40% of average $5,000 tuition (29 July).** Ian Aird told the Gold Coast conference: "We are growing to the point of being about 20% lower than we were 20 years ago" and "We have lost 20 years' worth of investment in this sector." Phil Honeywood (CEO, IEAA) matched the VET sector's grant rate at 60% and said the policies had cost Australia its entire Latin American pipeline: "We're saying no to VET but we desperately need the skills." ([The PIE, 29 July](https://thepienews.com/weve-lost-20-years-of-growth-australian-elicos-and-vet-sectors-hit-out-at-visa-policy/))

- **Home Affairs Assistant Secretary Noble frames tightened approvals as a "quality correction" that is "here to stay"; IEAA's Honeywood exposes the gap between allocations and actual grants; offshore higher education grant rate now 78%, down from 86–87% (29 July).** Noble told the same session: "There hasn't actually been any change in policy. What we are looking at...is a correction that is here to stay," citing a 14-day average offshore processing time. Honeywood countered: "There's a theory of an enrolment number allocation each year, and then it's the reality that visas are just not being approved." Adelaide University DVC (External Engagement) Jessica Gallagher described the combined effect of managed growth caps and visa unpredictability as a "beautiful double whammy." ([The PIE, 29 July](https://thepienews.com/australias-visa-quality-correction-leaves-sector-seeking-greater-certainty/))
