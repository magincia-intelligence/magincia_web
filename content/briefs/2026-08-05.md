## Top of the brief

- **Clare and King turn the first sod on CQUniversity's new $77.5 million Cairns CBD campus, which will deliver 58 courses across allied health, engineering, nursing, and medical imaging when it opens in 2028 (4 August).** The four-storey facility is backed by $77.5 million from the Albanese Government and is designed to expand tertiary access in Far North Queensland, where the relocation barrier has historically limited higher education participation. Clare: "This is going to help more young people in Far North Queensland become the nurses, engineers and front line health workers the region needs." Senator Nita Green: "When people have the opportunity to study close to home, they're more likely to stay home." ([Clare, 4 August](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/sod-turn-for-central-queensland-university-cairns-cbd-campus/))

## Funding & system architecture

- **Clare and Butler announce $30 million to fund 50 additional Commonwealth-supported medical university places from 2028, spread across ANU, UQ, Newcastle, Macquarie, Notre Dame, UNSW, QUT, and UWA; the allocation brings total new medical places since 2022 to 358, with the broader package adding 1,800 extra medical students annually when fully implemented (3 August).** The new places emphasise primary care training and increased general practice rotations; 2,100 doctors are now commencing government-funded GP training annually, supported by $1 billion in five-year grants to ACRRM and RACGP and $1.36 billion over five years for Rural Health Multidisciplinary Training. Butler: "More training places will ensure more young doctors work in primary care and in every corner of the country." ([Clare, 3 August](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/albanese-government-to-fund-50-additional-medical-university-places-to-boost-gp-workforce/))

## International education

- **Queensland secondary school principals convened by The PIE identify five structural challenges for K-12 international education: agent trust and transparency, teacher shortages constraining international cohort growth, undisclosed student mental health issues, the risks and opportunities of newly approved offshore delivery, and rising competitive pressure from international school systems globally (4 August).** Queensland private schools recently gained regulatory approval for transnational delivery; Michael Bos (Moreton Bay Colleges) cautioned that success "hinges on choosing the right overseas partners." Joanne Sheehy (A.B. Paterson College) noted that international families compare Australian schools against systems worldwide, demanding contemporary learning experiences. ([The PIE, 4 August](https://thepienews.com/lessons-from-queenslands-k-12-international-leaders/))
