## Top of the brief
- **Norton finds no identifiable new Commonwealth Grant Scheme funding to finance the government's higher education expansion targets — estimating at least 37,500 additional fully-funded Commonwealth Supported Places are needed just to replace lost over-enrolment volume, at a cost of roughly $500 million before any net growth is achieved (10 August).** The 2026 MYEFO budget write-down reduced CGS expenditure by $645.5 million to $7.7 billion; Norton identifies only approximately 28,700 discretionary CSPs for ATEC to work with in 2027 — about 15,000 from Job-ready Graduates policy reallocation and roughly 13,700 from automatic cuts to under-enrolled universities whose 2028 allocation will be capped at the lower of their 2027 domestic profile or their 2026 actuals. Against Clare's announced 16,000 new places per year from 2027–2029 (rising to 19,000 from 2030), Norton concludes: "The government is setting itself a double challenge — not just delivering more domestic student places but simultaneously reducing 'over-enrolments.'" The central contradiction: the government cannot simultaneously phase out the approximately 60,000 current over-enrolled places, grow total enrolment toward the 80% tertiary attainment target, and avoid new taxpayer spending — yet no new CGS appropriation has been secured from Cabinet. ([Norton, 10 August](https://andrewnorton.id.au/2026/08/10/how-will-the-government-finance-more-higher-education-enrolments/))

## International education
- **Panellists at The PIE Live Asia Pacific 2026 put the realistic addressable market for premium offshore branch campuses in India at only 40–45 million households earning approximately AUD $100,000 per year, and noted roughly 20% of graduates from India-based programs remain unemployed one year after completion — a caution for Australian providers evaluating transnational expansion (10 August).** James Cook University's "one JCU" strategy — prioritising student fit and outcomes over headcount — was cited as a contrasting model; Lisa Williams cautioned that institutions entering India reactively face "an incredible amount of risk." Malaysia's model of public-private TNE partnerships, which accommodates international students without displacing domestic enrolments, was highlighted as a lower-risk alternative pathway. ([The PIE, 10 August](https://thepienews.com/5-things-we-learned-about-multi-university-campuses-and-tne-at-the-pie-live-asia-pacific/))
