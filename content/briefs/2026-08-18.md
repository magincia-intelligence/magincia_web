## Top of the brief

- **Clare rejects Senator Pauline Hanson's characterisation of international education as a "farce," citing a sector still 13% below pre-pandemic enrolment levels, a National Planning Level of 295,000, visa fees raised from approximately $700 to $2,500, and a near-50% fall in net overseas migration since the government's reforms took effect (17 August).** Hanson had alleged international students use visas "as a backdoor to get into the country." Clare acknowledged problem actors — "There are shonks and there are dodgy providers who try and make a quick buck" — but framed the managed growth settings as evidence of a regulated sector: the University of Sydney is now capped at 50% international enrolment, and most international students return home after study. The $3.5 billion Universities Accord investment in domestic reform was presented as directly funded by international student fees. ([The PIE, 17 August](https://thepienews.com/clare-rejects-hansons-farce-claim-as-he-defends-international-education-reforms/))

## International education

- **Thirty US senators led by Senator Padilla urged Secretary of State Marco Rubio on 11 August to expedite student visa processing, citing 63% of US institutions expecting enrolment declines, a 9.5% projected overall fall, a 62% drop in visas granted to Indian nationals, and a $43 billion annual economic contribution at risk (17 August).** The senators called for extended interview waivers for returning vetted students and a resumption of monthly visa issuance data — suspended since September 2025 — within 30 days. Sustained US disruption adds to the theoretical demand pool for Australian providers; Australia's own 295,000 NPL, $2,500 student visa fee, and 42% India refusal rate remain the binding constraints on how much of that demand can be absorbed. ([The PIE, 17 August](https://thepienews.com/us-senators-call-for-faster-study-visa-processing/))

## Sector data

- **Standing figures:** 2026 NPL 295,000 (+25,000 on 2025); visa refusal rates 69% Nepal, 42% India (early 2026); domestic commencements 413,133 (+4.3% YoY); $50m over-enrolment fund; $50m Structural Adjustment Fund.
