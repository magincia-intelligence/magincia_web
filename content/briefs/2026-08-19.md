## Top of the brief

- **Clare at the AFR Higher Education Summit announces ATEC will allocate approximately 36,000 additional Commonwealth Supported Places — 20,000 to over-enrolled universities and 16,000 distributed across the system — alongside guaranteed places for students from low-income families and regional areas, needs-based funding modelled on the school system (Charles Sturt University projected to receive $200–500 million over a decade), standardised TAFE-to-university credit recognition to be completed by year-end, and TEQSA civil penalties increased from $40,000 to over $2 million (18 August).** Clare: "If you have got what it takes you will get a place." Professor Liz Johnson's teaching quality report — recommending mandatory teacher accreditation, professional learning standards, and a new Higher Education Teaching Hub — was also released at the summit. The 36,000-place commitment directly addresses the financing gap Norton identified on 10 August: it reconciles the phase-out of approximately 60,000 over-enrolled places against Clare's previously announced 16,000 new CSPs per year. ([Clare, 18 August](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/address-to-the-afr-higher-education-summit/))

## Funding & system architecture

- **Clare opens consultation on amendments to the Australian National University Act 1991, responding to TEQSA advice; proposed changes include equal numbers of elected and ministerially-appointed Council members, mandatory First Nations representation and gender balance, transfer of Nominations Committee appointment authority to the ANU Council, and addition of the Academic Board Chair as an ex officio Council member — with legislation expected later in 2026 (18 August).** Clare: "These proposed amendments respond to the advice of the Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA)." The release is the latest step in ANU's governance restructure following the Coaldrake-chaired Chancellor selection panel and TEQSA voluntary undertaking. ([Clare, 18 August](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/reforms-to-the-legislation-governing-the-australian-national-university/))

## International education

- **Approximately 1,500 predominantly Indian graduates from Portage College-linked programs in Canada had Post-Graduation Work Permit applications refused after IRCC ruled their non-credit programs ineligible; a 22-day Calgary protest ended on 12 August after CBSA identified 12 attendees as potentially inadmissible, with affected students having paid CAD$15,000–$25,000 for programs institutions had represented as PGWP-qualifying (18 August).** IRCC maintained its June 2024 guidance update "clarified an existing requirement rather than introducing a new rule"; Alberta Premier Danielle Smith: "A student visa is a temporary document to study in Canada. That was always understood." Affected graduates are challenging PGWP refusals in federal court. The episode — institutions making post-study work eligibility representations subsequently repudiated by immigration authorities — is a direct cautionary comparator for Australian providers communicating Temporary Graduate visa settings under managed growth constraints. ([The PIE, 18 August](https://thepienews.com/cbsa-checks-end-international-student-pgwp-protest-in-canada/))
