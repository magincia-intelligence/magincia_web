## Top of the brief

- **Clare announces the allocation of the $50 million Structural Adjustment Fund to ten universities through a competitive ATEC process, with James Cook University ($7.5M) and the University of Southern Queensland ($6.5M) receiving the largest individual grants, and the remaining $31M shared across the University of Canberra, University of Tasmania, University of the Sunshine Coast, University of New England, Australian Catholic University, CQUniversity, La Trobe University, and Federation University at $4–5M each (25 August).** The fund is designed to support the transition to the Managed Growth Funding and Needs-based Funding System — including investment in uni-TAFE pathways, degree apprenticeships, and technology — and is separate from the $3.6 billion in additional university funding committed over the decade under the Universities Accord legislation. Clare: "Universities create opportunity for Australians and build the skilled workforce we need. This funding will help universities, a lot of them in the regions, to keep delivering." ([Clare, 25 August](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/funding-to-strengthen-universities-as-reforms-roll-out/))

## Funding & system architecture

- **Universities Australia CEO Luke Sheehy, in an opening statement to the Senate inquiry into the Universities Accord (Opening the Doors of Opportunity) Bill 2026, raises four legislative concerns: the bill contains no growth floor guaranteeing total places cover existing student loads; ministerial power to impose conditions on individual universities outside mission-based compacts is excessive; international student commencement controls should be set nationally through ATEC rather than via ministerial direction to individual institutions; and needs-based funding should use a student's permanent address at course commencement, not current postcode (24 August).** Sheehy: "Australia needs more people going to university...opening the doors to more students should never mean handing government the keys to our universities. Universities are autonomous institutions. They are not departments of state." ([Universities Australia, 24 August](https://universitiesaustralia.edu.au/media-item/opening-statement-inquiry-into-the-universities-accord-opening-the-doors-of-opportunity-bill-2026/))

## International education

- **Education Centre of Australia (ECA) has partnered with Wuhan University to deliver an Australian IT diploma within China, enabling Chinese students to obtain an Australian qualification without leaving the country — one of the first such offshore delivery arrangements under the transnational framework (25 August).** The deal extends the offshore delivery model being explored by Queensland private schools and positions ECA ahead of peers in a market where onshore Chinese student numbers remain below pre-pandemic levels. ([The PIE, 25 August](https://thepienews.com/eca-strikes-deal-with-wuhan-university-to-deliver-australian-qualification-in-china/))
