## Top of the brief

- **Clare announces the third round of the $160 million Commonwealth Teaching Scholarships Program, awarding 1,000 scholarships valued at $40,000 (undergraduate, four years) or $20,000 (postgraduate, two years) from a pool of more than 3,300 applicants.** Around 70% of recipients achieved an ATAR above 80 or equivalent, 33% come from regional, rural, or remote areas, and 6% are First Nations students; recipients must commit to teaching in public schools or early learning settings for the duration of the scholarship, with a $2,000 top-up available for final-year placements in remote communities. ([Clare, 26 August](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/third-round-of-40000-commonwealth-teaching-scholarships/))

## Funding & system architecture

- **Norton analyses two government amendments incorporated into the Universities Accord (Opening the Doors of Opportunity) Bill 2026: a floor clause requiring ATEC to guarantee a minimum number of Commonwealth Supported Places per public university.** A structural gap remains: "ATEC has no direct power to add conditions to the allocations it makes," meaning conditions can only flow through funding agreements — a more cumbersome mechanism than direct allocation conditions. ([Norton, 24 August](https://andrewnorton.id.au/2026/08/24/government-amendments-to-its-student-places-capping-bill/))

## Sector data

- **Standing figures:** 2026 NPL 295,000 (+25,000 on 2025); visa refusal rates 69% Nepal, 42% India (early 2026); domestic commencements 413,133 (+4.3% YoY); $50m over-enrolment fund; $50m Structural Adjustment Fund.

## Regulator

- **Universities Australia chair Carolyn Evans, delivering her State of the Sector address at the National Press Club on 25 August as she departs as Griffith University vice-chancellor for a role at the University of Melbourne, warned that any policy decision to further reduce international student numbers must be accompanied by a plan for the financial consequences — and called on the government to consider legislating protection for university autonomy.** Evans: "A lot of public discourse likes to describe this as universities being greedy, risky, and addicted to international student funding. We are, in truth, forced by government policy to be reliant on it." And: "Having autonomous universities is also an inherent good — a critical protection against creeping authoritarianism and populist pressure. I am calling on the government to consider legislative protection for university autonomy." ([The PIE, 26 August](https://thepienews.com/australia-carolyn-evans-warns-of-fallout-from-further-international-student-cuts/))
