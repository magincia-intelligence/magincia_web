## Top of the brief

- **Appearing on Sunrise on 10 September, Clare identified digital screen reduction and maths curriculum reform from kindergarten to year 2 as the next two reform priorities following this week's PISA results, with Victoria separately announcing classroom screen time limits of 90 minutes daily in primary schools and two hours in secondary schools.** Clare: "The more kids are on screens at school, the more likely they are to fall behind." The interview also gave the PISA data an absolute framing: Australian 15-year-olds have recorded their worst-ever scores on the assessment across approximately 91 countries, with current students performing roughly two years below the level of those tested two decades ago, even while Australia retains an above-OECD-average ranking. ([Clare, 10 September](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/television-interview-with-natalie-barr-sunrise-thursday-10-september-2026/))

## International education

- **Canada is consulting provinces on linking Post-Graduation Work Permit access to labour market needs — asking institutions whether restrictions should apply to all graduates immediately upon announcement — as a separate increase to proof-of-funds requirements takes effect (CAD $23,448 from 1 September, up 2.6% on the prior threshold) (10 September).** Any further restriction on Canadian post-study work rights would widen the gap with Australia's Temporary Graduate visa settings, maintained under the managed growth framework as a structural differentiator; Australia's 42% India visa refusal rate and 295,000 NPL remain the binding constraints on how much of any redirected demand can be absorbed. ([The PIE, 10 September](https://thepienews.com/canada-set-to-tighten-rules-for-pgwp-linking-access-to-labour-market-needs/))

- **EC English announced it cannot accept new student arrivals at any of its 25 global schools this weekend, citing "unforeseen operational circumstances," with its Bristol school already closed in June and its Montreal location closing at end of September — the latest provider-level disruption in a global ELT market that fell 23% in student weeks in 2025 (10 September).** EC English has no Australian campuses, but the announcement has produced immediate agent concern about operational resilience across the English-language provider landscape, and is a further signal of stress in a market in which Australia's own ELICOS student week decline was the steepest of the eight major study destinations last year. ([The PIE, 10 September](https://thepienews.com/ec-english-halts-new-student-arrivals-worldwide-this-weekend/))
