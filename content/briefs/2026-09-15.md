## Top of the brief
- **One Nation has released a migration policy pledging net-negative migration in its first three years of government, including cuts to more than 750,000 temporary visas and a reduction in student visa numbers from approximately 590,000 (total current stock) to 350,000 — with a cap of around 100,000 new student arrivals annually — prompting immediate responses from the university sector (14 September).** Pauline Hanson: "We will take back control of who comes here and how many we accept." Luke Sheehy (Universities Australia): "A vote for dramatically fewer international students is a vote for a poorer, less skilled Australia." IHEA chief executive Peter Hendy noted international education is Australia's largest services export and the independent higher education sector alone contributes $3 billion in annual tax revenue. The pledge represents a significant escalation of political competition to restrict international student numbers, with proposed stock levels well below the government's current National Planning Level of 295,000 new commencements per year. ([The PIE, 14 September](https://thepienews.com/one-nation-pledges-net-negative-migration-and-750000-cut-to-temporary-visas/); [Universities Australia, 14 September](https://universitiesaustralia.edu.au/media-item/slashing-international-student-numbers-would-do-lasting-damage-to-australia/))

## International education
- **EC English has entered formal insolvency proceedings and closed all 25 schools across seven countries with immediate effect on 14 September — four days after halting new student arrivals on 10 September — citing failure to secure additional investment, with more than 40,000 current students affected globally.** UK students (approximately 370 across four schools) are being supported under English UK's Student Emergency Support scheme; Languages Canada is coordinating alternative placements for 375 Canadian students with more than 25 member schools. EC English has no Australian campuses, but its collapse — following a Bristol closure in June and Montreal at end of September — is the most significant provider failure in global ELT in recent years and reinforces the structural pressures identified in the 2025 BONARD data (global ELT student weeks down 23%). CEO Andrew Mangion: "We recognise that this is extremely difficult and disappointing news." ([The PIE, 14 September](https://thepienews.com/ec-english-closes-all-global-schools/))
