## Top of the brief

- **Clare and Walsh announce that legislation locking in a 15% pay rise for early childhood educators has passed, with more than 80% of eligible services already receiving government payments covering the increase, 20,000 additional educators having entered the workforce since the initial pay rise announcement, and job vacancies in the sector down 31%.** Full-time educators receive an additional $255 per week; early childhood teachers receive an additional $410 per week; $3.6 billion in government funding underpins the commitment, with mandatory safety standard enforcement beginning July 2027 and 95% of services already meeting those standards. Clare: "Caring for and teaching children is some of the most important work in the country. Our early educators deserve every cent they get." ([Clare, 15 September](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/15-pay-rise-for-early-educators-locked-in/))

## International education

- **Education New Zealand reports the sector's export earnings reached NZ$4.98 billion in the year to June 2026 — surpassing the pre-pandemic peak of NZ$4.403 billion (2019) — with an estimated 99,000 international student enrolments projected for 2026, an 8% year-on-year increase in Jan–April enrolments, and university growth of 14% over the same period.** ENZ chief David Downs: "Students today want more than a certificate. They want practical learning, good job prospects." The recovery reflects a structural preference shift rather than purely pandemic rebound — in a destination that displaced Australia as agents' top-ranked study destination for the first time in 2026. ([The PIE, 15 September](https://thepienews.com/new-zealands-rebound-signals-shift-in-student-demand/))

- **US colleges including Harvard, Yale, Princeton and others have halted new internship approvals for international students following August 2026 DHS guidance tightening Curricular Practical Training eligibility — requiring that practical training be mandatory and integral to a degree program rather than elective — affecting approximately 130,000 students who use CPT annually and compounding on the 34% F-1 issuance decline in 2025 and the Duration of Status rule effective 15 September.** One university reported an 8% enrolment decline in the current cycle, with further falls projected for 2027. ([The PIE, 15 September](https://thepienews.com/us-colleges-restrict-internships-as-cpt-rules-tighten/))
