## Top of the brief

- **Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke announced that most international students and temporary graduate visa holders will no longer be able to bring dependants to Australia, with course-change restrictions also limiting students to upward qualification progression only and requiring new visa applications for any provider or course change — effective prospectively.** In 2025–26, 70% of 10,448 dependant visa grants went to applicants from Nepal, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, and Sri Lanka; exemptions cover research students, Pacific Islands, Timor-Leste, ASEAN, and foreign affairs/defence sponsored students, accounting for approximately 10,000–11,000 annual grants against a total of 45,000–49,000. Burke: "We will no longer be adding to the stock of students automatically being able to bring family members over." Phil Honeywood (IEAA): "We will definitely lose talented young people as a result of today's announcement." Luke Sheehy (Universities Australia): "Cutting migration too hard comes with consequences for our economy, our universities, our workforce and the services Australians rely on." ([The PIE, 17 September](https://thepienews.com/australia-to-restrict-student-dependants-amid-warnings-of-talent-loss/); [Universities Australia, 17 September](https://universitiesaustralia.edu.au/media-item/migration-settings-must-match-australias-economic-needs/))

## International education

- **Norton analyses Burke's secondary visa changes, finding the 67,549 secondary student visa holders onshore are joined by a further 73,704 secondary temporary graduate visa holders also in scope, and projects the total demand impact will exceed the secondary visa numbers because primary visa holders weighing a four-to-five-year family separation are likely to avoid Australia entirely rather than come without dependants; South Asian-reliant universities face the greatest exposure.** Norton notes that a Nine newspapers poll found 48% of respondents supporting a ban on family visas for international students, reflecting what he characterises as a "dramatically changed political climate on migration," and observes that the changes appear to apply only to new applications — not current secondary holders already in Australia. ([Norton, 17 September](https://andrewnorton.id.au/2026/09/17/tony-burkes-changes-to-student-visa-policy-first-draft-on-secondary-visa-holders/))

- **PIE columnist Neil Fitzroy observes that Australia's International Education and Skills Strategic Framework is arriving after the migration rules that will shape the sector, arguing that "signals travel faster than policy" — pointing to the 2024 precedent of provider-level caps that failed legislatively yet still drove market responses — and that individual restrictions are accumulating without a coherent overarching model for what Australia wants from international education.** The column identifies ASEAN and Pacific exemptions as foreign policy signals embedded within an otherwise demand-suppression posture. ([The PIE, 17 September](https://thepienews.com/australia-where-the-bloody-hell-are-you/))
