## Top of the brief
- **Universities Australia has absorbed the University Chancellors Council, bringing chancellors and vice-chancellors under a single national body, with three chancellor directors — John Hood (Flinders University), Diane Smith-Gander (University of Western Australia), and Sandra McPhee (Southern Cross University) — joining its ten-member board; the UCC will cease operations on 31 October 2026, ahead of new national governance standards taking effect in January 2027 and a requirement for other higher education providers to comply by July 2027 (28 September).** The change follows a Senate inquiry into university transparency and executive pay; UCC convenor John Pollaers will not continue on the new board. Evans: "Universities have a responsibility to maintain high standards, and this change reflects our shared commitment." ([Universities Australia, 28 September](https://universitiesaustralia.edu.au/media-item/university-leadership-comes-together-to-strengthen-national-voice/); [The PIE, 28 September](https://thepienews.com/australias-chancellors-join-university-peak-body-as-governance-rules-tighten/))

## International education
- **Norton finds that 6,659 direct student visa holders received points-tested permanent residence visas in 2024–25 — with the subclass 190 Skilled Nominated visa accounting for 48% of grants to former international students — while expressions of interest for the subclass 190 reached 164,525 in August 2026, a 59% increase since June 2025, as the temporary graduate visa holder population has doubled since late 2022 while the total points-tested planning level has fallen to 70,700 places in 2026–27 from 82,900 in 2025–26 (28 September).** Norton identifies a structural mismatch between surging demand and contracting permanent residence supply, with cuts to the subclass 491 regional visa driving the overall decline even as subclass 189 and 190 allocations increased. ([Norton, 28 September](https://andrewnorton.id.au/2026/09/28/points-tested-visas-and-international-students-demand-and-supply/))
