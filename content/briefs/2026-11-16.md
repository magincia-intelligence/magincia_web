## Top of the brief

- **ATEC has released the 2027 Mission Based Compacts and Allocations Outcomes Report — marking the commission's first annual allocation cycle — confirming additional Commonwealth-supported student places and establishing the new framework for system stewardship in higher education; Universities Australia welcomed the report as "an important step in higher education reform," calling for compacts that are "genuinely negotiated, respect institutional autonomy and foster a diverse and high-performing higher education system" (12 November).** ATEC will progress the longer-term 2028–31 compact framework and in future assume responsibility for international student place allocations, with Universities Australia noting those decisions must employ "transparent, sector-developed methodologies." ([Universities Australia, 12 November](https://universitiesaustralia.edu.au/media-item/atec-report-marks-important-step-in-higher-education-reform/))

- **Clare announced on 13 November that fully funded Commonwealth-supported places at Australian universities will reach 624,590 in 2027 — an increase of 3.8 per cent from approximately 601,500 in 2026 — with every university in Australia to receive additional capacity under compacts agreed with the Australian Tertiary Education Commission, the first full allocation cycle conducted under the Universities Accord (Opening the Doors of Opportunity) Act 2026.** Regional universities (the seven RUN members plus James Cook, Newcastle, and Wollongong) and universities in every state and territory are included; ACU and Notre Dame, which operate across multiple states, are excluded from the aggregate totals. Clare: "Next year more Australians will go to university than ever before. This will mean we have more teachers, more nurses, more scientists, more engineers. It will mean more Australians getting the skills they need and that Australia needs." ([Ministers, 13 November](https://ministers.education.gov.au/clare/more-australians-getting-shot-university-ever-extra-places-right-across-country))

## International education

- **Adelaide University Deputy Vice Chancellor International Jessica Gallagher, writing in The PIE on 12 November, argues that Australia's accumulating visa restrictions — increased charges, slower processing, significant refusals in key markets, and dependant restrictions — threaten not only the economic value of international education but Australia's multicultural identity, citing 2026 commencements tracking 8% below the 295,000 NPL and the mathematical tension between the government's 225,000 annual migration target and the sector's planning level.** South Australian international students and families participate in community volunteering at a rate of approximately 30%. Gallagher: "Australia can legitimately decide that migration levels need to change...But none of these things require abandoning the idea that cultural diversity is an Australian strength." ([The PIE, 12 November](https://thepienews.com/migration-is-essential-to-education-and-the-australian-way-of-life/))

- **Reporting from AIEC 2026 — held at Sydney's International Convention Centre from 27–30 October with 1,750 delegates — shows overseas higher education commencements falling 4.9% year on year in early 2026, with IDP research released at the conference finding Australia's first-choice share among prospective students slipping to 24% after a near one-quarter fall in six months; more than four in five surveyed students said they were comparing multiple destinations (The PIE, 10 November).** Assistant minister Julian Hill acknowledged that high refusal rates were deterring students in key South Asian markets and said he wanted "grant rates to improve and greater certainty for everyone," noting that Home Affairs is consulting on changes to the Genuine Student assessment and financial-capacity evidence rules, including a possible requirement that funds be held in an Australian bank account before lodging a visa application. IEAA CEO Phil Honeywood: "We've got this conundrum of profit motive versus the public good." Clare, speaking at the conference: "Not everyone can come here to study. Not everyone can afford it. But we can come to them." ([The PIE, 10 November](https://thepienews.com/aiec-2026-australia-rethinks-its-international-education-promise/))
