## Top of the brief
- **Minister Jason Clare, addressing the TEQSA 2026 Conference on 17 November, announced that TEQSA reform legislation will be introduced to Parliament the following week — making compliance with the Threshold Standards a condition of registration for the first time, giving TEQSA power to direct providers to fix non-compliance, raising civil penalties to more than $2 million, and making the Chief Commissioner the Accountable Authority with the CEO reporting to the Commission rather than operating independently; Clare also confirmed he has asked the ARC for advice on his powers to revoke existing grants on research security grounds, following his refusal of 13 grant applications in May.** University enrolments have risen from 274,000 starters when Clare took office to an expected 354,000 in 2027 — a 30% increase. Clare: "Talent is everywhere. It's opportunity that's not." ([Jason Clare, 17 November](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/address-to-teqsa-conference-2026/))

## International education
- **The US District Court for Massachusetts ruled on 16 November to set aside the DHS Duration of Status rule in its entirety, finding it arbitrary and capricious — DHS failed to weigh costs and benefits, did not substantiate fraud or national security claims, and the rule's academic study restrictions exceeded statutory authority; the ruling preserves the longstanding Duration of Status framework under which F-1 students remain in status for the duration of their program rather than being limited to fixed admission periods of up to four years.** The ruling removes a significant push factor away from the US at a moment when Open Doors data (covered yesterday) already show 18% fewer new US enrolments; for Australia, the 295,000 NPL and 69% Nepal / 42% India refusal rates remain the binding constraint on how much redirected demand can be absorbed. ([The PIE, 17 November](https://thepienews.com/federal-court-scraps-trumps-four-year-limit-on-international-students-stays/))
