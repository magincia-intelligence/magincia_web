## Top of the brief
- **Minister Jason Clare, addressing the La Trobe University roundtable on Prevention of Gender-based and Sexual Violence on 24 November, reported that only 24 of the 42 universities assessed had been found sufficiently compliant with the National Higher Education Code to Prevent and Respond to Gender-based Violence — which came into force on 1 January 2026 for universities and extends to non-university higher education providers from 1 January 2027 — and that the National Student Ombudsman, established with legally binding recommendation powers, is in place to enforce the Code; the government is separately investing $78 million to teach consent and respectful relationships in schools.** The 2021 National Student Safety Survey — the benchmark underpinning the reform — found one in 20 students reported sexual assault in a university setting, one in six reported sexual harassment, and one in two said they were not heard when they made a complaint. ([Jason Clare, 24 November](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/address-to-la-trobe-roundtable-prevention-of-gender-based-and-sexual-violence-at-uni/))

## Funding & system architecture
- **The Department of Education published an infographic on 23 November outlining the proposed Australian Teaching and Learning Commission — a national body intended to bring together expertise in curriculum, assessment, teaching, research, and education technology to provide more connected, evidence-based support for teachers, schools, and education systems — covering the commission's proposed functions, benefits for educators and learners, and next steps for implementation.** ([Department of Education, 23 November](https://www.education.gov.au/schooling/resources/teaching-and-learning-commission-infographic))

## International education
- **A US District Court temporarily blocked DHS enforcement of its August 2026 memos restricting curricular practical training (CPT) for F-1 students on 17 November, with Judge Julia Kobick ruling the five plaintiff organisations — including the Association of American Universities and NAFSA — were likely to succeed on the merits; more than 120 international students at Columbia University had already been denied CPT authorisation before the ruling, and stakeholders warned lasting damage to student confidence in the US may outlast the legal win.** Sanjay Laul (MSM Unify): "One ruling brings down the temperature. It does not change the weather." The ruling follows the separate 16 November decision scrapping the Duration of Status rule; for Australia, Open Doors 2026 projected new US enrolments down a further 15% in fall 2026, but Australia's NPL of 295,000 and visa refusal rates of 69% (Nepal) and 42% (India) remain the binding constraint on absorbing redirected demand. ([The PIE, 24 November](https://thepienews.com/us-cpt-ruling-welcomed-but-concerns-over-student-confidence-remain/))
