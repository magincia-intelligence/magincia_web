## Top of the brief

- **Universities Australia CEO Luke Sheehy, writing in The Australian on 25 November, warned that international student visa restrictions have driven an 8% fall in student numbers, a 9% drop in enrolments and a 12% decline in new course commencements in the first eight months of 2026 versus the same period in 2025; Assistant Minister Julian Hill's own estimate is that new overseas student commencements at public universities will fall around 18% in 2026, costing "about $2 billion in lost university revenue over three years"; the standard visa application charge has "more than tripled in two years to $2,500," and by June 2026 almost 1 in 4 student visa applications was being refused.** International education contributes $55 billion to the economy and supports around 250,000 Australian jobs, with more than $30 billion flowing through local spending. Sheehy: "The government can't keep banking the political benefits of fewer visas while leaving Australians to foot the bill." ([Universities Australia, 25 November](https://universitiesaustralia.edu.au/media-item/universities-australia-warns-international-student-visa-cuts-will-cost-jobs-and-billions-in-revenue/))

## Funding & system architecture

- **The Department of Education published on 24 November the government's formal response to the Senate Education and Employment Legislation Committee inquiry into the Universities Accord (Opening the Doors of Opportunity) Bill 2026, addressing the additional recommendations in the Australian Greens' Dissenting Report and Senator David Pocock's Minority Report; the response was provided through Senate debate and covers recommendations not incorporated when the bill passed with 43 amendments on 12–13 October.** ([Department of Education, 24 November](https://www.education.gov.au/about-department/resources/government-response-senate-education-and-employment-legislation-committee-inquiry-2026))
