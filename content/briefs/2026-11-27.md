## Top of the brief

- **Minister Jason Clare introduced the Universities Accord (Strengthening Governance) Bill 2026 to Parliament on 26 November, fulfilling his commitment at the 17 November TEQSA Conference; the bill makes compliance with the Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) a formal condition of registration for the first time, grants TEQSA power to issue infringement notices, raises the maximum civil penalty from roughly $40,000 to over $2 million, and requires TEQSA to consider systemic risks including racism and governance failures when assessing providers; the ANU Act is separately amended to mandate merit-based appointment of the Chancellor and Vice-Chancellor, a publicly available Code of Conduct for Council members, and First Nations representation on Council.** University commencements have risen from 274,000 when Clare took office to approximately 354,000 expected in 2027 — a 30% increase — with Clare framing the governance bill as the accountability complement to the access expansion. Clare: "At the moment TEQSA has a sledgehammer and a feather and not much in-between." ([Jason Clare, 26 November](https://jasonclare.com.au/media/legislation-to-strengthen-university-governance/))

- **Phil Honeywood, CEO of the International Education Association of Australia, told The PIE on 26 November that 2026 international commencements in Australia are likely to reach only 250,000 — well short of the 295,000 NPL — with data as of 20 November showing only 44,900 prospective students holding active student visas against the 2027 planning level; public universities had reached just 67% of their 2026 allocation compared with 150% for other higher education providers; Ministerial Direction 115 applies tiered processing priority cuts to offshore applications once providers reach 80% (Priority 2) and 115% (Priority 3) of their indicative allocation.** Honeywood also flagged that onshore transfers will decline sharply in 2027 following the planned introduction of a new transfer visa from July. Honeywood: "With few political friends, 2027 is shaping up to be another incredibly challenging year for Australia's beleaguered international education sector." ([The PIE, 26 November](https://thepienews.com/australia-faces-challenging-2027-as-early-international-student-figures-emerge/))

## International education

- **UK study visa refusals reached their highest rolling rate in over a decade — 6.4% — with applications withdrawn in the peak recruitment quarter rising more than 370% year-on-year as of 26 November, compounding pressure across South Asian and African source markets that overlap directly with Australia's international student recruitment pipeline.** ([The PIE, 26 November](https://thepienews.com/uk-study-visa-refusals-hit-highest-rate-in-over-a-decade-as-withdrawals-soar/))

- **Nepal's new mandatory NPR 2.5 million deposit requirement for licensed education agencies risks forcing smaller operators out of business and increasing reliance on unregistered advisers — a risk with direct relevance to Australia given Nepal's 69% visa refusal rate and its position as a major source market.** ([The PIE, 26 November](https://thepienews.com/nepals-new-rules-could-push-responsible-education-agencies-out-of-business/))
